# Bio-inspired Thermal Management Systems: From Insect Cuticle to Hierarchical Composites

**Nature Materials, Vol. 22, pp. 892-904, 2023**

*Yuki Tanaka¹, Christopher J. Evans², Priya Sharma³*

¹ Kyoto University, Institute for Chemical Research  
² University of Cambridge, Department of Engineering  
³ Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, Department of Metallurgical Engineering

## Abstract

We report a bio-inspired approach to thermal interface materials that mimics the hierarchical structure of insect cuticle. Using chitin nanofibers as self-assembly templates, we create hierarchical porous boron nitride (BN) architectures that, when infiltrated with silicone matrix, achieve exceptional thermal conductivity (15+ W/m·K) with low thermal contact resistance. The biomimetic manufacturing process — involving chitin-templated BN self-assembly followed by template pyrolysis — produces materials with through-thickness thermal transport channels that outperform conventional randomly-filled composites.

## 1. Introduction

Nature has evolved sophisticated thermal management strategies over millions of years. Insect cuticle, in particular, presents a remarkable hierarchical architecture: chitin nanofibers arranged in helicoidal stacking patterns create ordered structures spanning multiple length scales from nanometers to millimeters.

We hypothesized that mimicking this architecture with thermally conductive materials could create TIMs with unprecedented performance.

## 2. Bio-inspired Manufacturing Process

### 2.1 Chitin Nanofiber Template Preparation
Chitin nanofibers (diameter: 20-50 nm, length: 1-5 μm) were extracted from crab shells via acid hydrolysis and mechanical defibrillation. The nanofibers were dispersed in water (2 wt%) and cast into films via vacuum filtration, producing ordered templates.

### 2.2 BN Nanosheet Self-Assembly on Chitin Template
Exfoliated BN nanosheets (lateral size: 0.5-2 μm, thickness: 2-5 nm) were adsorbed onto chitin nanofiber surfaces via electrostatic interaction. The BN nanosheets spontaneously wrapped around the chitin fibers, creating a core-shell structure.

### 2.3 Template Pyrolysis
The chitin template was removed by controlled pyrolysis (600°C, 2h, N₂ atmosphere), leaving behind a hierarchical porous BN architecture that retained the ordered structure of the original template.

### 2.4 Silicone Infiltration
The porous BN scaffold was infiltrated with liquid silicone (PDMS, viscosity: 100 cP) under vacuum, followed by thermal curing (120°C, 4h).

## 3. Structural Characterization

### 3.1 Hierarchical Architecture
SEM and TEM analysis confirmed:
- Nanoscale: BN nanosheets on fiber surfaces (2-5 nm sheet thickness)
- Microscale: Tubular BN channels (diameter: 50-100 nm) from template removal
- Mesoscale: Ordered arrangement of channels following original chitin fiber orientation

### 3.2 Porosity and Density
- BN scaffold porosity: 65-75%
- Final composite density: 1.8-2.1 g/cm³
- BN content: 45-55 wt%

## 4. Thermal Performance

### 4.1 Thermal Conductivity
| Sample | Through-thickness (W/m·K) | In-plane (W/m·K) | Anisotropy |
|--------|--------------------------|-------------------|------------|
| Random BN/silicone (50 wt%) | 4.2 | 5.8 | 0.72 |
| Bio-inspired (50 wt%) | 15.3 | 8.1 | 1.89 |

### 4.2 Thermal Contact Resistance
Bio-inspired TIM showed 60% lower contact resistance compared to conventional BN/silicone composites of equivalent filler loading.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Mechanism
The hierarchical structure creates continuous through-thickness thermal pathways — analogous to how insect cuticle creates structural continuity across length scales. The ordered BN channels provide direct heat conduction paths, eliminating the phonon scattering bottlenecks present at random filler-filler interfaces.

### 5.2 Scalability
The process is inherently scalable: chitin is abundant (second most common biopolymer after cellulose), BN nanosheet exfoliation is well-established, and template pyrolysis requires only standard furnace equipment.

## 6. Conclusions

Bio-inspired thermal management materials using chitin nanofiber templates offer a new paradigm for TIM design. The hierarchical porous BN/silicone composites achieve thermal conductivities (>15 W/m·K) that significantly exceed conventional randomly-filled composites, while maintaining the compliance and processability required for practical TIM applications.

The key innovation is the use of biological templates (chitin nanofibers) for directed self-assembly of BN nanosheets, followed by template removal to create ordered thermal transport channels.
