GOVERNING LAW REFERENCE NOTES
==============================

Prepared by: International Legal Advisory Team
Date: March 2024
Re: MPA-2024-0892 (HuaTech / Thornton-Clarke)

1. ENGLISH LAW CONSIDERATIONS
-----------------------------
- Under English common law, a side letter may modify the terms of a master agreement
  provided it is executed with valid consideration and clear intent.
- The Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999 may be relevant if third-party
  beneficiaries are identified.
- Under English law, where there is a conflict between documents forming part of the
  same transaction, the later-in-time document generally prevails unless the master
  agreement contains a clear hierarchy clause.
- LCIA arbitration is well-established for international commercial disputes with
  average proceedings lasting 12-18 months.
- English courts generally uphold choice of law clauses unless they contravene
  mandatory public policy.

2. PRC LAW CONSIDERATIONS
-------------------------
- Under PRC Contract Law (now Civil Code, effective 2021), supplementary agreements
  (补充协议) are valid modifications of the original contract when signed by
  authorized representatives.
- Article 510 of the PRC Civil Code: where a supplementary agreement conflicts with
  the original contract, the supplementary agreement prevails as the later expression
  of the parties' intent.
- CIETAC is China's primary international arbitration institution; awards are
  enforceable under the New York Convention (1958).
- PRC courts may refuse to enforce a foreign governing law clause if it conflicts
  with PRC mandatory rules (e.g., labor law, environmental regulations, consumer
  protection).
- Foreign exchange controls: payments in CNY to/from foreign parties require SAFE
  (State Administration of Foreign Exchange) approval or registration.

3. CONFLICT OF LAWS ANALYSIS
-----------------------------
- The master agreement (Art. 11.3) states: "this Agreement shall prevail unless the
  side letter expressly states otherwise with reference to the specific Article being
  modified."
- The side letter (Art. 1.2) states: "本附函中的约定在与主协议冲突时，以本附函为准"
  (In case of conflict between this side letter and the master agreement, this side
  letter shall prevail.)
- Key conflict: Both documents contain a "prevail" clause favoring themselves.
- Under English law: the specific hierarchy clause in Art. 11.3 of the master
  agreement would likely be given effect, meaning the side letter must expressly
  reference the specific Article being modified.
- Under PRC law: Article 510 of the Civil Code would likely give priority to the
  later-in-time document (the side letter) as it represents the latest agreed intent.

4. JURISDICTIONAL CONFLICT
--------------------------
- The master agreement designates English law + LCIA arbitration (London).
- The side letter designates PRC law + CIETAC arbitration (Shenzhen).
- This creates a potential "battle of jurisdictions" – if a dispute arises, each
  party may commence proceedings in their preferred forum.
- Under English law, an anti-suit injunction may be sought to prevent proceedings
  elsewhere.
- Under PRC law, CIETAC may accept jurisdiction based on the side letter clause.
- The New York Convention (to which both UK and China are signatories) generally
  requires courts to refer parties to arbitration where a valid agreement exists,
  but conflicting agreements create uncertainty.

5. RISK FACTORS
---------------
- Payment term conflict (60 days vs 30 days) creates cash flow uncertainty for
  the Buyer.
- Liability cap increase (5M vs 10M CNY) significantly increases Seller's exposure.
- Jurisdictional conflict is the highest-risk item – may render dispute resolution
  provisions unenforceable.
- Currency change (USD to CNY) introduces FX risk for both parties.
- Warranty period reduction (24 to 12 months) reduces Buyer protections.
- Delivery term change (CIF London to FOB Shenzhen) shifts significant risk/cost
  to Buyer.

6. APPLICABLE INTERNATIONAL INSTRUMENTS
----------------------------------------
- United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (CISG)
  – potentially applicable unless expressly excluded
- New York Convention on Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards (1958)
- ICC Incoterms 2020
- Hague Principles on Choice of Law in International Commercial Contracts (2015)
