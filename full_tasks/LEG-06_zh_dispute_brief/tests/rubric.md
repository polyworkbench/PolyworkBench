# Rubric: LEG-06

## Dimension 1: Legal Analysis Quality (Weight: 30%)
- **Excellent (9-10)**: Comprehensive Chinese litigation brief covering arbitration seat (Singapore), applicable law (English), dispute amount ($2.35M), force majeure claim analysis, ICC case reference
- **Good (7-8)**: Most key legal elements covered with proper Chinese legal analysis
- **Adequate (5-6)**: Basic legal analysis with some elements
- **Poor (1-4)**: Minimal legal content
- **Zero (0)**: No litigation brief

## Dimension 2: Chinese Language Quality (Weight: 25%)
- **Excellent (9-10)**: Professional Chinese legal writing (>15% CJK ratio), proper legal terminology, structured sections, formal register
- **Good (7-8)**: Good Chinese legal content with structure
- **Adequate (5-6)**: Some Chinese content present
- **Poor (1-4)**: Minimal Chinese
- **Zero (0)**: No Chinese content

## Dimension 3: Evidence Analysis (Weight: 20%)
- **Excellent (9-10)**: Cross-language evidence synthesis from English and Russian sources, timeline reconstruction, contract clause citations
- **Good (7-8)**: Evidence from both sources with some citations
- **Adequate (5-6)**: Basic evidence gathering
- **Poor (1-4)**: Minimal evidence analysis
- **Zero (0)**: No evidence analysis

## Dimension 4: Strategy Recommendations (Weight: 15%)
- **Excellent (9-10)**: Actionable litigation strategy with specific recommendations, risk assessment, and cost-benefit analysis
- **Good (7-8)**: Strategy with recommendations and some risk analysis
- **Adequate (5-6)**: Basic strategy suggestions
- **Poor (1-4)**: Minimal strategy content
- **Zero (0)**: No strategy recommendations

## Dimension 5: Completeness (Weight: 10%)
- **Excellent (9-10)**: All required deliverables present with answer.json containing correct key facts
- **Good (7-8)**: Most deliverables present
- **Adequate (5-6)**: Some deliverables missing
- **Poor (1-4)**: Major gaps
- **Zero (0)**: No output

## Language Quality Assessment
- Target language output must be professional quality Chinese
- No English fallback in the litigation brief
- Technical terminology must follow Chinese international arbitration conventions
