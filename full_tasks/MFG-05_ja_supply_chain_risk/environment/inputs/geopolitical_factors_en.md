# Geopolitical Risk Factors for Supply Chain Assessment

## Overview
This document outlines key geopolitical risk factors affecting supply chains across China, South Korea, and Vietnam as of Q1 2024.

## China (中国)

### Trade Policy Risks
- **US-China trade tensions**: Ongoing tariffs (25% on many electronic components), export controls on advanced semiconductors and manufacturing equipment
- **Entity List restrictions**: Growing list of Chinese companies subject to US export controls; risk of secondary sanctions affecting suppliers
- **Dual circulation policy**: Chinese government push for domestic substitution may affect long-term supply relationships

### Regulatory Risks
- **Data security law**: Requirements for data localization may complicate supply chain visibility systems
- **Environmental regulations**: Increasing enforcement of pollution controls; factory shutdowns during high-pollution periods in northern regions
- **Labor regulations**: Rising labor costs (8-10% annual increase in manufacturing wages)

### Operational Risks
- **COVID-19 legacy**: While lockdowns have ended, zero-COVID experience revealed vulnerability of concentrated manufacturing
- **Power supply**: Periodic electricity rationing in manufacturing regions (Guangdong, Jiangsu) during summer peaks
- **Natural disasters**: Typhoon exposure in southern coastal manufacturing zones (Guangdong, Fujian)

### Risk Level: MEDIUM-HIGH
- Likelihood of disruption: 3-4/5
- Potential impact: 3-5/5 depending on supplier concentration

## South Korea (한국)

### Geopolitical Risks
- **North Korea tensions**: Periodic escalation creates market uncertainty; direct military threat remains low probability but high impact
- **US-China competition**: Korea caught between largest trade partner (China) and security ally (US); semiconductor export controls create policy dilemmas
- **Japan-Korea relations**: Historical tensions occasionally affect trade (2019 export control dispute as precedent)

### Economic Risks
- **Won volatility**: KRW/USD fluctuation impacts procurement costs (±10% in 12 months)
- **Concentration in chaebols**: Heavy reliance on large conglomerates means fewer alternative suppliers for high-end components
- **Aging workforce**: Manufacturing labor shortage emerging, automation investment increasing costs

### Operational Risks
- **Semiconductor cycle**: Korean suppliers heavily exposed to semiconductor demand cycles
- **Energy dependence**: 94% energy import dependence creates cost vulnerability
- **Natural disasters**: Limited exposure (occasional typhoons, earthquakes rare)

### Risk Level: MEDIUM
- Likelihood of disruption: 2-3/5
- Potential impact: 3-4/5 for high-tech components

## Vietnam (Việt Nam)

### Political/Regulatory Risks
- **Political stability**: One-party system provides policy continuity but limited transparency
- **Regulatory uncertainty**: Rapidly evolving regulations as economy develops; inconsistent enforcement across regions
- **Anti-corruption campaigns**: Periodic government campaigns can disrupt business operations and delay approvals

### Infrastructure Risks
- **Transportation**: Road and port infrastructure still developing; congestion at major ports (Hai Phong, Cat Lai)
- **Power supply**: Electricity shortages in northern Vietnam during dry season; rolling blackouts reported in 2023
- **Industrial parks**: Quality varies significantly; some parks have inadequate waste treatment

### Workforce Risks
- **Labor turnover**: High turnover rates (20-30% annually) in manufacturing zones
- **Skill gaps**: Limited availability of experienced quality engineers and technical managers
- **Labor disputes**: Occasional wildcat strikes, particularly around Tet holiday period

### Operational Risks
- **Natural disasters**: Flooding risk in central/northern regions; typhoon season June-November
- **Supply chain maturity**: Supporting industries less developed; many raw materials still imported from China
- **Quality consistency**: Younger manufacturing base; quality systems less mature than China/Korea

### Risk Level: MEDIUM
- Likelihood of disruption: 3/5
- Potential impact: 2-3/5 (lower value components, alternative sources available)

---

## Cross-Regional Risks

### Maritime Disruption
- South China Sea tensions affect shipping routes between all three countries
- Container shortage and shipping cost volatility (post-2021 normalization but still elevated)
- Key chokepoint: Strait of Malacca for oil supplies to all three countries

### Technology Decoupling
- US CHIPS Act and export controls creating parallel technology ecosystems
- "Friend-shoring" trend may benefit Vietnam/Korea but complicate China sourcing
- Increasing pressure for supply chain transparency and origin documentation

### Climate and ESG
- All three countries face increasing climate-related supply chain disruptions
- Growing ESG disclosure requirements from customers (EU CSRD, SEC climate rules)
- Carbon border adjustment mechanisms may increase costs for energy-intensive manufacturing

## Recommended Assessment Frequency
- China suppliers: Monthly monitoring, quarterly deep review
- Korea suppliers: Quarterly monitoring, semi-annual review
- Vietnam suppliers: Monthly monitoring (infrastructure/labor), quarterly review
